THE STORY BEHIND ARKAY

It started at my grandfather’s bedside.

“Water.”

He had something to say.

My grandfather spent 25 days in the ICU following a tracheostomy. During that time, speaking became difficult. I kept thinking about how hard it could be to ask for something as simple as water.

Could a movement carry a word?

I started with what a person already does when they try to speak. Their lips move. So does their jaw. I wanted to see whether those movements could help us tell which word they were trying to say.

Start with a word that matters.

That question became Arkay. We began with a small English vocabulary for everyday needs. The prototype suggests likely matches, then gives a caregiver a chance to confirm the word and play it aloud.

HOW ARKAY WORKS

From a silent attempt to a spoken word.

Try to speak

A patient mouths a word such as “water.”

Capture the movement

A camera tracks the lips and a sensor picks up movement at the jaw.

Review the suggestions

Arkay offers likely matches from its trained vocabulary. A caregiver can confirm the intended word.

Play the word aloud

Arkay speaks the confirmed word so the care team can hear it.

THE PROTOTYPE TODAY
